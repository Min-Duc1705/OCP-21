### Question 1
**When you use `GROUP BY ROLLUP(region_id, country_id, department_id)` with 3 independent columns, how many total grouping levels will Oracle generate?**

* A. 3 grouping levels.
* B. 4 grouping levels.
* C. 8 grouping levels.
* D. 6 grouping levels.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** For $N$ simple expressions in `ROLLUP`, Oracle generates **$N + 1$** grouping levels by progressively dropping columns from right to left. With $N = 3$ (`region_id`, `country_id`, `department_id`), `ROLLUP` generates $3 + 1 =$ **4 grouping levels**:
    1. `(region_id, country_id, department_id)`
    2. `(region_id, country_id)`
    3. `(region_id)`
    4. `()` (Grand Total)
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** $8$ levels ($2^3$) is the count for `CUBE(region_id, country_id, department_id)`.
</details>

### Question 2
**When you use `GROUP BY CUBE(region_id, country_id, department_id)` with 3 independent columns, how many total grouping levels will Oracle generate?**

* A. 4 grouping levels.
* B. 6 grouping levels.
* C. 8 grouping levels.
* D. 9 grouping levels.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:** For $N$ independent expressions in `CUBE`, Oracle generates **$2^N$** grouping levels (every possible combination). With $N = 3$, it generates $2^3 =$ **8 grouping levels**:
    1. `(region_id, country_id, department_id)`
    2. `(region_id, country_id)`
    3. `(region_id, department_id)`
    4. `(country_id, department_id)`
    5. `(region_id)`
    6. `(country_id)`
    7. `(department_id)`
    8. `()` (Grand Total)
  * **A, B, D are incorrect:** They do not match the $2^N$ formula for `CUBE`.
</details>

### Question 3
**Which of the following grouping levels is GENERATED by `GROUP BY CUBE(department_id, job_id)` but is NOT GENERATED by `GROUP BY ROLLUP(department_id, job_id)`?**

* A. The detail level `(department_id, job_id)`.
* B. The department subtotal level `(department_id)`.
* C. The job subtotal level `(job_id)`.
* D. The grand total level `()`.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:**
    * `ROLLUP(department_id, job_id)` drops columns from right to left, generating only 3 levels: `(department_id, job_id)`, `(department_id)`, and `()`. It **does not generate `(job_id)`**.
    * `CUBE(department_id, job_id)` generates all $2^2 = 4$ combinations: `(department_id, job_id)`, `(department_id)`, **`(job_id)`**, and `()`.
  * **A, B, D are incorrect:** All three of these levels are generated by both `ROLLUP` and `CUBE`.
</details>

### Question 4
**Examine the following Partial Rollup clause: `GROUP BY department_id, ROLLUP(job_id, manager_id)`. Which grouping levels are generated by this clause?**

* A. `(department_id, job_id, manager_id)`, `(department_id, job_id)`, `(department_id)`, and `()`
* B. `(department_id, job_id, manager_id)`, `(department_id, job_id)`, and `(department_id)`
* C. `(department_id, job_id, manager_id)`, `(job_id, manager_id)`, `(job_id)`, and `()`
* D. `(department_id, job_id)` and `(department_id, manager_id)`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** Because `department_id` is outside `ROLLUP(...)`, this is a **Partial Rollup**:
    * `ROLLUP(job_id, manager_id)` produces 3 sets: `(job_id, manager_id)`, `(job_id)`, and `()`.
    * Combining the fixed `department_id` column with each of those 3 sets yields **3 grouping levels**:
      1. `(department_id, job_id, manager_id)`
      2. `(department_id, job_id)`
      3. `(department_id)`
    * Notice that **no overall Grand Total `()` row** is generated because `department_id` is always present.
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** Option A mistakenly includes an overall Grand Total `()`.
</details>

### Question 5
**Examine the following query that uses a Composite Column inside `ROLLUP`. How many grouping levels are generated, and what are they?**

```sql
SELECT a, b, c, SUM(sales)
FROM sales_tab
GROUP BY ROLLUP(a, (b, c));
```

* A. 4 levels: `(a, b, c)`, `(a, b)`, `(a)`, and `()`
* B. 3 levels: `(a, b, c)`, `(a)`, and `()`
* C. 2 levels: `(a, b, c)` and `()`
* D. 3 levels: `(a, b, c)`, `(b, c)`, and `()`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** Enclosing `(b, c)` in extra parentheses inside `ROLLUP(a, (b, c))` makes `(b, c)` a single **Composite Column** unit. Thus, `ROLLUP` sees only **2 units**—`a` and `(b, c)`—and produces $2 + 1 =$ **3 grouping levels** (dropping units from right to left):
    1. Keep both units: `(a, b, c)`
    2. Drop the right unit `(b, c)` as a whole: `(a)` *(never splitting `b` and `c`, so `(a, b)` is skipped!)*
    3. Drop `a`: `()` (Grand Total)
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** Option A describes `ROLLUP(a, b, c)` without a composite column.
</details>

### Question 6
**The clause `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS (department_id, job_id)` (note: without inner parentheses around `department_id, job_id`) is equivalent to which of the following?**

* A. `SELECT department_id, job_id, SUM(salary) FROM employees GROUP BY department_id, job_id`
* B. A `UNION ALL` of two queries: one grouped by `department_id` (with `job_id` as `NULL`) and another grouped by `job_id` (with `department_id` as `NULL`).
* C. `SELECT department_id, job_id, SUM(salary) FROM employees GROUP BY ROLLUP(department_id, job_id)`
* D. `SELECT department_id, job_id, SUM(salary) FROM employees GROUP BY CUBE(department_id, job_id)`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** In `GROUPING SETS (department_id, job_id)`, the comma separates two distinct single-column grouping sets, equivalent to `GROUPING SETS ((department_id), (job_id))`. Therefore, Oracle computes **2 independent subtotal levels**:
    1. Grouped by `(department_id)`
    2. Grouped by `(job_id)`
    (Neither the combined `(department_id, job_id)` detail level nor the Grand Total `()` is computed!)
  * **A is incorrect:** Grouping by both columns together requires **nested parentheses**: `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((department_id, job_id))`.
  * **C, D are incorrect:** `ROLLUP` produces 3 levels and `CUBE` produces 4 levels.
</details>

### Question 7

### Question 8
**The clause `GROUP BY ROLLUP(a, b)` is 100% equivalent to which `GROUPING SETS` clause below?**

* A. `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS (a, b, ())`
* B. `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((a, b), (a), (b), ())`
* C. `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((a, b), (a), ())`
* D. `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((a, b), (b), ())`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:** `ROLLUP(a, b)` drops columns from right to left, generating 3 grouping sets: `(a, b)`, `(a)`, and `()`. Written using `GROUPING SETS`, this is `GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((a, b), (a), ())`.
  * **A is incorrect:** Produces `(a)`, `(b)`, and `()`, missing `(a, b)`.
  * **B is incorrect:** This is equivalent to `CUBE(a, b)`.
  * **D is incorrect:** Keeps `(b)` instead of `(a)`.
</details>

### Question 9
**When does the `GROUPING(column_name)` function return `1`?**

* A. When the underlying value of `column_name` stored in the base table is `NULL`.
* B. When the current row is a normal detail group row with a non-null value.
* C. When `column_name` is aggregated over (excluded from the grouping set of the current row) and its displayed `NULL` is a super-aggregate `NULL` generated by `ROLLUP`, `CUBE`, or `GROUPING SETS`.
* D. When there are 2 or more duplicate rows in the same group.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:**
    * **`GROUPING(col) = 1`**: Indicates a **super-aggregate row** where `col` has been rolled up (the displayed `NULL` represents *all values* of `col`).
    * **`GROUPING(col) = 0`**: Indicates `col` is part of the current grouping level (even if the actual stored data value in the table for that group happens to be `NULL`!).
  * **A, B, D are incorrect:** If a base table row has a genuine `NULL` in `department_id`, its detail group row still returns `GROUPING(department_id) = 0`.
</details>

### Question 10
**Examine the query below. On a subtotal row for each `department_id` (where all `job_id` values in that department are rolled up together), what are the values of `GROUPING(department_id)`, `GROUPING(job_id)`, and `GROUPING_ID(department_id, job_id)`, respectively?**

```sql
SELECT department_id,
       job_id,
       GROUPING(department_id) AS g_dept,
       GROUPING(job_id)        AS g_job,
       GROUPING_ID(department_id, job_id) AS gid,
       SUM(salary)
FROM employees
GROUP BY CUBE(department_id, job_id);
```

* A. `g_dept = 1`, `g_job = 0`, `gid = 2`
* B. `g_dept = 0`, `g_job = 1`, `gid = 1`
* C. `g_dept = 1`, `g_job = 1`, `gid = 3`
* D. `g_dept = 0`, `g_job = 0`, `gid = 0`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** On a subtotal row grouped by `(department_id)`:
    * `department_id` is part of the grouping set $\implies$ `GROUPING(department_id) = 0`.
    * `job_id` is aggregated over (super-aggregate `NULL`) $\implies$ `GROUPING(job_id) = 1`.
    * `GROUPING_ID(department_id, job_id)` treats the bits `0` and `1` as binary `01`, which converts to decimal **$0 \times 2^1 + 1 \times 2^0 = 1$**.
  * **A is incorrect:** `1, 0, 2` (binary `10`) occurs on the subtotal row for `(job_id)`.
  * **C is incorrect:** `1, 1, 3` (binary `11`) occurs on the Grand Total `()` row.
  * **D is incorrect:** `0, 0, 0` (binary `00`) occurs on the detail `(department_id, job_id)` row.
</details>

### Question 11
**In a query with `GROUP BY CUBE(a, b, c)`, what integer value does `GROUPING_ID(a, b, c)` return on the Grand Total `()` row?**

* A. `3`
* B. `6`
* C. `7`
* D. `8`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:** On the Grand Total `()` row, all 3 columns `a`, `b`, and `c` are aggregated over:
    * `GROUPING(a) = 1`, `GROUPING(b) = 1`, `GROUPING(c) = 1`.
    * The bit vector is binary `111`.
    * Converting binary `111` to decimal: $1 \times 2^2 + 1 \times 2^1 + 1 \times 2^0 = 4 + 2 + 1 =$ **`7`**.
  * **A, B, D are incorrect:** For $N$ columns in `GROUPING_ID`, the Grand Total row always returns $2^N - 1$ ($2^3 - 1 = 7$).
</details>

### Question 12
**In a query using `GROUP BY ROLLUP(a, b, c)`, if you add the filter `HAVING GROUPING_ID(a, b, c) > 0`, which rows will be retained in the final result?**

* A. Only the detail rows `(a, b, c)` are retained, and all subtotal/total rows are excluded.
* B. The detail rows `(a, b, c)` are excluded, and only the subtotal rows (`(a, b)`, `(a)`) and the Grand Total row (`()`) are retained.
* C. Only the single Grand Total row `()` is retained.
* D. It fails with an error because `GROUPING_ID` is not allowed in the `HAVING` clause.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** `GROUPING()`, `GROUPING_ID()`, and `GROUP_ID()` are valid in `SELECT`, **`HAVING`**, and `ORDER BY` clauses.
    * On detail rows `(a, b, c)`, the bit vector is `000` $\implies$ `GROUPING_ID(a, b, c) = 0`.
    * On all subtotal rows (`001 = 1`, `011 = 3`) and the Grand Total row (`111 = 7`), `GROUPING_ID` is strictly $> 0$.
    * Thus, `HAVING GROUPING_ID(a, b, c) > 0` filters out the detail rows and keeps **only the subtotal and grand total rows**.
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** Using `GROUPING_ID` in `HAVING` is a standard technique for filtering specific aggregation levels.
</details>

### Question 13
**When you combine two `ROLLUP` clauses separated by a comma in the same `GROUP BY` clause (Concatenated Grouping Sets), such as `GROUP BY ROLLUP(a, b), ROLLUP(c, d)`, how many total grouping levels are generated?**

* A. The sum of the two `ROLLUP` levels: $(2 + 1) + (2 + 1) = 6$ grouping levels.
* B. The Cartesian product of the two `ROLLUP` levels: $(2 + 1) \times (2 + 1) = 3 \times 3 = 9$ grouping levels.
* C. Always $2^4 = 16$ grouping levels.
* D. It fails with a syntax error because a `GROUP BY` clause can contain at most one `ROLLUP` keyword.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** When multiple `ROLLUP`, `CUBE`, or `GROUPING SETS` expressions are separated by a comma in a `GROUP BY` clause (**Concatenated Grouping Sets**), Oracle computes the **Cartesian product (cross-product)** of their grouping sets:
    * `ROLLUP(a, b)` generates 3 sets: `(a, b)`, `(a)`, `()`.
    * `ROLLUP(c, d)` generates 3 sets: `(c, d)`, `(c)`, `()`.
    * Combining each set on the left with each set on the right produces $3 \times 3 =$ **9 grouping levels**: `(a,b,c,d)`, `(a,b,c)`, `(a,b)`, `(a,c,d)`, `(a,c)`, `(a)`, `(c,d)`, `(c)`, and `()`.
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** A comma *outside* `GROUPING SETS(...)` multiplies combinations, whereas a comma *inside* `GROUPING SETS(...)` unions them.
</details>

### Question 14
**In a composite grouping query such as `GROUP BY a, ROLLUP(a, b)`, the grouping level `(a)` is generated twice (duplicate grouping sets). Which condition should you add to the `HAVING` clause to eliminate duplicate summary rows while keeping the first occurrence of each grouping level?**

* A. `HAVING GROUPING(a) = 0`
* B. `HAVING GROUPING_ID(a, b) = 0`
* C. `HAVING GROUP_ID() = 0`
* D. `HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT a) = 1`

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:** The `GROUP_ID()` function (which takes no arguments) distinguishes duplicate grouping sets produced by a `GROUP BY` specification:
    * The first occurrence of any grouping set returns `GROUP_ID() = 0`.
    * Duplicate occurrences of the same grouping set return `1`, `2`, etc.
    * Adding `HAVING GROUP_ID() = 0` removes all duplicate summary rows.
  * **A, B, D are incorrect:** Neither `GROUPING` nor `GROUPING_ID` distinguishes between the first and second copy of the same `(a)` grouping level.
</details>

### Question 15
**Examine the following SQL statement using `LISTAGG`. Why does this statement fail with a syntax error?**

```sql
SELECT department_id,
       LISTAGG(first_name, ', ') AS employee_names
FROM employees
GROUP BY department_id;
```

* A. Because the `LISTAGG` function must always include the `WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY ...)` clause.
* B. Because the delimiter `', '` cannot contain spaces.
* C. Because `LISTAGG` cannot be used with a `GROUP BY` clause.
* D. Because `LISTAGG` only accepts `NUMBER` columns, not `VARCHAR2` columns.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **A**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **A is correct:** In Oracle SQL, `LISTAGG` is an ordered-set aggregate function, so the **`WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY ...)` clause is mandatory** to specify the concatenation order. Omitting it raises `ORA-02000: missing WITHIN keyword`.
  * **B, C, D are incorrect:** Even if order does not matter, you must write `LISTAGG(first_name, ', ') WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY NULL)`.
</details>

### Question 17
**When `LISTAGG` concatenates so many rows that the resulting string exceeds the maximum `VARCHAR2` length (4000 bytes in standard mode) and no `ON OVERFLOW` clause is specified, what happens by default? And how can you safely truncate the string starting in Oracle 12cR2?**

* A. By default, Oracle silently truncates the string to 4000 bytes without raising an error.
* B. By default, Oracle raises `ORA-01489: result of string concatenation is too long`; to truncate safely without an error, include the `ON OVERFLOW TRUNCATE` clause inside `LISTAGG`.
* C. By default, Oracle automatically converts the return type of `LISTAGG` to `CLOB`.
* D. By default, Oracle returns `NULL`.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** The default behavior of `LISTAGG` is `ON OVERFLOW ERROR`, which raises runtime error **`ORA-01489: result of string concatenation is too long`** when the byte limit is exceeded. Starting in Oracle 12cR2, specifying `ON OVERFLOW TRUNCATE` inside `LISTAGG` truncates the string cleanly at the last complete item and appends an indicator without raising an error.
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** `LISTAGG` never automatically switches its return type to `CLOB`.
</details>

### Question 18
**When you write `LISTAGG(first_name, ', ' ON OVERFLOW TRUNCATE) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY first_name)` without specifying a custom truncation indicator string or `WITH/WITHOUT COUNT`, what default suffix does Oracle append to a truncated string?**

### Question 19

```sql
SELECT first_name,
```

### Question 20
**Examine the `PIVOT` query below against the `EMPLOYEES` table (which contains columns `EMPLOYEE_ID`, `FIRST_NAME`, `HIRE_DATE`, `DEPARTMENT_ID`, `JOB_ID`, `SALARY`). Why does this query fail to group the output into one summary row per `DEPARTMENT_ID`, returning hundreds of separate rows instead?**

```sql
SELECT *
FROM employees
PIVOT (
    SUM(salary)
    FOR job_id IN ('IT_PROG' AS it, 'SA_REP' AS sa, 'AD_VP' AS vp)
);
```

* A. Because `SUM(salary)` is not allowed inside `PIVOT`.
* B. Because `PIVOT` implicitly groups by **all columns from the source table that are not referenced inside the `PIVOT` clause** (including `EMPLOYEE_ID`, `FIRST_NAME`, `HIRE_DATE`, etc.), making every employee a separate group!
* C. Because a `GROUP BY department_id` clause is missing at the end of the `PIVOT` query.
* D. Because only 3 job IDs are listed in the `IN` clause.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **B**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **B is correct:** This is the #1 real-world and exam trap with `PIVOT`! `PIVOT` does not use an explicit `GROUP BY` clause; instead, it performs an **implicit `GROUP BY` on all remaining columns in the source query** that are not part of the `PIVOT (AGG(...) FOR ...)` clause. Querying `FROM employees` directly includes `EMPLOYEE_ID` (unique per row), `FIRST_NAME`, etc., in the grouping key!
    * **Best practice:** Always wrap the source in an inline view (`FROM (SELECT department_id, job_id, salary FROM employees)`) so only the desired grouping columns are projected.
  * **A, C, D are incorrect:** You cannot add an explicit `GROUP BY` to the same query block as `PIVOT`.
</details>

### Question 21
**In a `PIVOT (SUM(salary) FOR department_id IN (...))` clause, when are you allowed to use a subquery (`SELECT DISTINCT department_id FROM ...`) or the `ANY` keyword inside the `IN (...)` list?**

* A. Always, in any standard `PIVOT` query.
* B. Only when using **`PIVOT XML`** syntax (in which case the pivoted output is returned as an `XMLTYPE` column).
* C. Only when combined with `UNPIVOT`.
* D. Never in Oracle SQL.

<details>

  * **B is correct:** In a standard relational `PIVOT` query, the SQL compiler must know the exact output columns at **parse/compile time**, so the `IN (...)` list must consist of static constant literals. Only **`PIVOT XML (...)`** allows `IN (SELECT ...)` or `IN (ANY)` because the dynamic columns are encapsulated inside a single `XMLTYPE` column.

### Question 22
**In the `PIVOT` query below, **2 aggregate functions** and **3 pivot values** are specified, along with 1 grouping column (`dept_id`). How many total columns will the output result set contain?**

```sql
SELECT *
FROM (SELECT dept_id, job_id, salary FROM emp_summary)
PIVOT (
    SUM(salary) AS sum_s,
    COUNT(*)    AS cnt_e
    FOR job_id IN ('AD_VP' AS vp, 'IT_PROG' AS it, 'SA_REP' AS sa)
);
```

* A. 4 columns.
* B. 6 columns.
* C. 7 columns (`dept_id` + $3 \times 2 = 6$ pivoted columns).
* D. It fails because `PIVOT` allows at most 1 aggregate function.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 View Answer & Detailed Explanation</b></summary>

* **Correct Answer:** **C**
* **Detailed Explanation:**
  * **C is correct:** `PIVOT` supports multiple aggregate functions separated by commas (each aggregate must have an alias when more than one is used).
    * Number of generated pivot columns: $(\text{Values in } \text{IN}) \times (\text{Aggregate functions}) = 3 \times 2 = 6$ columns (`VP_SUM_S`, `VP_CNT_E`, `IT_SUM_S`, `IT_CNT_E`, `SA_SUM_S`, `SA_CNT_E`).
    * Plus the 1 implicit grouping column `DEPT_ID` $\implies$ **$1 + 6 = 7$ columns total**.
  * **A, B, D are incorrect:** They either forget the grouping column or fail to multiply the `IN` values by the number of aggregate functions.
</details>

### Question 23
**The `EMP_CONTACTS` table has a single row: `EMP_ID = 101`, `WORK_PHONE = '090111'`, `HOME_PHONE = NULL`, `CELL_PHONE = '098999'`. How many rows are returned when you execute the `UNPIVOT` query below?**

```sql
SELECT emp_id, phone_type, phone_num
FROM emp_contacts
UNPIVOT (
    phone_num
    FOR phone_type IN (work_phone AS 'WORK', home_phone AS 'HOME', cell_phone AS 'CELL')
);
```

* A. 3 rows (with the `'HOME'` row having `phone_num` as `NULL`).
* B. 2 rows (only the `'WORK'` and `'CELL'` rows; the `'HOME'` row is omitted because `UNPIVOT` defaults to `EXCLUDE NULLS`).

### Question 24
